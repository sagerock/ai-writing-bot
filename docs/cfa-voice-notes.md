WHO IS SPEAKING
The Center for Anthroposophy is a small, forty-year-old Waldorf teacher-education institute in Wilton, New Hampshire, with a global circle of alumni, faculty and friends. It writes as a community, never as a brand. Named people speak in the first person with a byline and a role: David Barham, Executive Director; Torin Finser, Development Director; Milan Daler, Administrator; program directors for their programs. When no person is named, the voice is "we" — the Center and its community together.

HOW IT ADDRESSES PEOPLE
Readers are friends and colleagues, addressed as such: "Dear Friends," "Dear Waldorf Colleagues, Alums, and Friends," "To our dear friends, alums and donors." Warmth is explicit and unembarrassed. Sign-offs match: "With warm regards," "Many thanks," "In Appreciation." Gratitude is stated often and sincerely.

WHAT IT SOUNDS LIKE
Reflective and unhurried. Sentences are complete and often long; paragraphs develop an idea rather than list points. It is comfortable with history and lineage — dates, founders, elders honored by name (Douglas Gerwin, John F. Gardner, David Sloan) — and with the movement's own vocabulary: renewal, striving, impulses, the human being in body, soul and spirit, a "cultural deed." Rudolf Steiner is quoted with care and context, never as decoration. Anthroposophy is explained for a colleague, not assumed or defended.

The frame is global and generous: participants "from all over the planet," the Center as "part of the global dialogue," conferences in Kassel and Toronto mentioned as naturally as Wilton. Humor is gentle and human — a beloved leader called "Captain Waldorf," an evening of "human warmth (and cakes and cookies!)."

PROGRAM AND INVITATION COPY
Practical, nourishing, inspiring — those three words recur and are the register. Verbs of restoration: rekindle, nurture, deepen, reconnect, step back, prepare thoughtfully. "The year ahead." "Whether you join us in person or online." Dates and places stated plainly and bolded: Wilton, NH; Keene, NH; Abbot Hill; June 27 – July 2. Invitations are open-handed: "tell us you're interested and we'll write to you when registration opens — there's no obligation and nothing to pay now." Appeals are brief and personal: "donations to CfA are possible year round," "the work of our CfA."

PROGRAMS ARE PROPER NOUNS
Renewal Courses, WHiSTEP (Waldorf High School Teacher Education Program), Kairos Institute, Explorations Online, Starlight Rays, Mentor Training, Waldorf Leadership Development, Building Bridges, the Antioch University partnership. Use the full names on first mention. Center & Periphery is the triannual newsletter; "News from Abbot Hill" is its recurring column.

WHAT IT NEVER DOES
No hype, no urgency tricks, no exclamation-heavy selling. No corporate or marketing vocabulary — no "unlock," "leverage," "game-changing," "solutions." No bullet-point memos where a paragraph would do. No assuming the reader is an insider, and no apologizing for the tradition either. Never speaks for a person who has not signed; drafts for a named writer are written in that person's first person and left for them to send.

WRITING FOR THE OFFICE
Board and committee reports: same warmth, plainer structure, numbers stated without spin, gratitude to the people behind them. Letters to parents and participants: clear on dates, money and next steps; kind about the rest. Social posts: one warm sentence and a real detail, not a slogan.
